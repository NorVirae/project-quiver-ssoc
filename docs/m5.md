# M5 Payloads and enclosure

**Deliverable:** secure payload attachment and enclosure assemblies with clear mechanical, power and data interfaces.

## Prepare

Collect **2111 side spacers, 2131 bottom spacers, 2112 quick-release plates, 3331 Attachment Interface PCBs** and **2411/2412 enclosure parts**, plus an inert training payload. Check the released CAD/BOM revision. M3 verifies power/data pinouts; attachment positions are not automatically electrically interchangeable.

## Work sequence

1. Identify the three attachment locations and the parts assigned to each.
2. Inspect manufactured parts using the approved dimensions and acceptance criteria.
3. Assemble spacers, plates, latches and enclosure parts in the specified order.
4. Fit the inert payload while the aircraft is isolated and properly supported.
5. Inspect engagement, retention, clearance, cable routing and strain relief.
6. Record payload mass and position. Agree supply and data requirements with M3, M4 and M6.

## Stop and ask

Stop for damaged prints, unclear load limits, inadequate retention or conflicting interfaces. Never infer a load rating from appearance or successful fit. Do not repair a structural print or conduct a load test without a separate approved method. All payload swaps require isolation.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Correct parts and revisions fitted.
- [ ] Retention and clearance satisfy the approved inspection method.
- [ ] No unapproved structural modification or repair.
- [ ] Payload mass and mounting position recorded.
- [ ] Mechanical, electrical and data interfaces accepted by receiving teams.
- [ ] Any remaining payload flight approval is explicitly outstanding.

## Show your understanding

Demonstrate latch inspection with an inert payload. Explain why the location of the payload matters as well as its mass.

**Submit:** fit and retention evidence, part inspection, mass/location and interface record. The supervisor accepts the build; attachment acceptance alone does not approve a flight with that payload.
