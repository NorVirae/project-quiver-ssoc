# Damaged equipment and emergency action

**Your outcome:** stop unsafe work, prevent reuse of defective equipment and give a useful report.

## An ordinary fault without immediate danger

1. Stop the activity. Make the area safe only within your training and authority.
2. Tell the supervisor promptly. Do not reconnect an item to see whether the fault repeats.
3. Mark the item **DO NOT USE** with its asset ID, observed fault, date and reporter.
4. The supervisor isolates it and assigns the correct quarantine or repair location.
5. Record the event and its effect on other assemblies. Only authorised staff release it back to service.

Do not return suspect tools, boards or cables to usable stock. Do not place a damaged battery in the ordinary equipment quarantine area.

## Smoke venting or fire

Raise the alarm, withdraw and evacuate by the safe route. Go to the assembly point and follow staff instructions. Do not carry burning or venting batteries, open an affected enclosure, attempt firefighting or re-enter to retrieve equipment.
If a fire blocks your exit and you must fight your way out, use a CO₂ or Dry Powder extinguisher:
Twist and pull the safety pin to snap the plastic seal (do not squeeze the handles while pulling).
Aim at the base of the fire, squeeze the lever, and sweep from side to side.

## Injury or electric shock

Alert staff immediately. Keep clear of electrical hazards. Do not touch a person still in contact with an electrical source. Trained responders isolate the danger and provide first aid. Use the posted university emergency process.

## Report near misses too

Report a dropped pack, unexpected motor movement, wrong connector, overheated lead or missing guard even when no injury occurred. Describe what you observed, what was connected, who was informed and what remains isolated. Do not assign blame or conceal an error.
