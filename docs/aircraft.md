# Handling the aircraft and changing payloads

**Your outcome:** move and work on the assembly without exposing people to rotating parts, trapped fingers or unintended power.

## Before moving the aircraft

Identify its weight, centre of gravity and approved lifting points. Clear the route and prepare a stable stand. Use the required number of people or lifting aid; do not lift by propellers, cables, sensors or unsupported covers.

The supervisor confirms isolation. Remove and account for every propeller before bench work. Support the aircraft so it cannot tip or fold unexpectedly.

## Folding arms and mechanical work

Inspect joints and latches using the approved drawing and method. Keep fingers clear of hinges and pinch points. Check cables through the permitted movement range. Never force a misaligned joint or alter a structural part to make it fit.

Protect openings against loose fasteners and wire offcuts. Count tools before closing covers. Record incomplete fasteners, curing joints and restrictions on the handover label.

## Payload changes

1. Obtain the integration lead's release and establish safe isolation.
2. Support the payload before releasing the attachment latch or connector.
3. Identify the approved attachment, power and data interfaces.
4. Fit the inert training payload and inspect retention, clearance and cable strain relief.
5. Record its mass, mounting position and outstanding checks.

This course requires isolation for every swap. A quick-release interface does not establish that live electrical connection is permitted. Fit and retention alone do not prove a payload is approved for flight.

## Powered checks

Only the integration lead's authorised operator energises the shared aircraft. Other teams remain clear and do not change wiring or parameters. Follow the written stop plan. If a motor moves unexpectedly, call STOP and step clear; do not grab it.
