# Charging with the TATTU TA7200Pro

**Your outcome:** understand the charging and storage controls and recognise when the session must stop.

## Before charging

**Demonstration pair:** the TATTU 4.0 14S 20 Ah smart battery and **TATTU TA7200Pro**, it's charger. Staff verify the actual pack variant, firmware and matching manufacturer lead. ACES and Molex variants are not interchangeable; never force or improvise an adapter.

The pack manual specifies a **60.2 V charging upper limit (4.3 V per cell)**. This is a verification limit, not an instruction to configure a generic charger. Do not apply a generic 4.35 V-per-cell LiHV profile.

Use the designated noncombustible, ventilated charging area and approved containment. Keep at least **30 cm between charger and pack, and between packs**; staff may require more. Do not improvise a sealed box.

Facilities staff must approve the TA7200Pro supply circuit and cable. Do not power it from a computer extension strip. Record pack ID, charger ID, operator, approved mode and start time.

## While charging

- A trained authorised person supervises throughout the charge cycle. Do not leave charging unattended.
- Observe the TATTU communication indicator, voltage, current and temperature. Do not select Fast mode to shorten a class.
- Report any alarm, unexpected heating, swelling, smell or damaged connection.
- For a normal stop, staff hold the Pause/Stop control as the manual directs and verify charging has stopped before disconnecting. For smoke, venting or fire, raise the alarm and withdraw; do not approach to unplug.
- Never change charger settings merely to clear a fault.

## Return to storage

Staff apply the TATTU manual's storage and battery-management procedure, record the storage state and set the next inspection date. Do not guess a storage voltage or bypass the smart-battery controls.

Protect terminals individually; separate packs so connections cannot short; record their condition and location. Store only in the approved battery location and containment, away from ordinary components and combustible stock. Damaged packs require separate staff-managed arrangements.

Never put batteries in general rubbish. Staff arrange disposal through the approved route.
